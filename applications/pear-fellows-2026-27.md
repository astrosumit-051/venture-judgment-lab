## What Pear says it selects for

Pear says it wants students who are motivated to support entrepreneurs, proactive, and able to think independently. It asks for evidence of an entrepreneurial network or industry expertise and willingness to help build the firm.

The application should therefore make four things concrete:

1. A real founder-support behavior, not general enthusiasm for startups.
2. A defensible area of expertise, grounded in repeated work or lived experience.
3. Access to a campus founder community and a useful plan for serving it.
4. Independent judgment: how the learner notices, evaluates, or helps something others may miss.

## Video structure after the Independent First Pass

Target runtime: 2 minutes 30 seconds. Speak naturally; do not read a dense essay.

| Time | Job of the section | Evidence to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:20 | Open with identity and a specific reason venture matters to you | One lived moment, not a generic career aspiration |
| 0:20–0:50 | Show founder-support behavior | What you did, for whom, and what changed |
| 0:50–1:20 | Demonstrate independent investment judgment | One evaluation, insight, or evidence-driven change of mind |
| 1:20–1:50 | Establish differentiated access or expertise | One industry lens plus one campus network edge |
| 1:50–2:15 | Explain what you will build for Pear | A feasible campus program, founder pipeline, or event you will own |
| 2:15–2:30 | Close with why Pear is the right apprenticeship now | Connect partner mentorship, live deal work, diligence, or thesis formation to a specific gap |

Avoid saying that Pear is a paid internship, that the learner has sourced or diligenced investments without defensible evidence, or that a founder is exceptional based only on school, résumé, charisma, or fundraising.
